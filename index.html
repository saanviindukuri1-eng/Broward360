TOOL 02 · DIGITAL SAFETY

Not sure if a message is real?

Help older adults and other residents spot common warning signs in suspicious texts and emails.

The image is processed in this browser by text-recognition software. For best results, use a clear screenshot with readable text.

Privacy tip: remove names, addresses, account numbers, verification codes, and other private details before checking a message.

Scam warning-sign check

Your results will appear here. This tool looks for common warning signs; it cannot prove whether a message is real or fake.

Never click a link, pay with gift cards or cryptocurrency, or share a password or one-time code because a message tells you to. Contact the organization using a phone number or website you find independently.

Prototype note: automated checks can make mistakes. If money or account access is at risk, ask a trusted person or contact the organization through its official channel. Text recognition uses a third-party library downloaded from the internet; the app itself does not send your image to a Broward360 server.

TOOL 03 · LEARN DIGITAL SAFETY

Digital Safety Coach

Practice spotting common scams with short examples. Choose the safest next step and learn why it helps.

🎭

Impersonation scams

A caller says they are from your bank and needs your password to “protect” your account.

Safer move: Hang up and call the bank using the number on your card or its official website—not a number the caller gives you.

📦

Fake delivery notices

A text says a package is delayed and asks you to pay a small fee through a link.

Safer move: Don’t tap the link. Check delivery status using the retailer’s app or the carrier’s official website you open yourself.

▦

Suspicious QR codes

A QR code on a random sticker or unexpected message promises a prize or asks you to sign in.

Safer move: Don’t sign in or enter payment details after scanning an unexpected code. Check where it leads before opening it.

🖥️

Tech-support scams

A pop-up claims your computer is infected and tells you to call a phone number immediately.

Safer move: Don’t call the pop-up number or grant remote access. Close the page and contact a trusted support provider independently.

TOOL 04 · PLAIN-LANGUAGE ACCESS

Make a confusing letter easier to understand.

Upload a photo or paste text from a utility bill, government notice, or service letter. Broward360 will extract text and highlight possible dates, amounts, and action items.

Use a clear, well-lit image. Crop or cover your name, address, account number, barcode, and other personal information first.

Your letter, simplified

Upload a photo or paste text, then select “Find key information.”

Important: This prototype does not use AI to understand the full meaning of a letter. It uses OCR plus simple JavaScript rules to flag possible dates, amounts, and action-related phrases. Check every result against the original document and contact the organization through independently verified official contact information. It cannot verify authenticity or provide legal, tax, or financial advice.

OCR uses Tesseract.js, the same text-recognition library as the Scam Checker. The library is downloaded from a third-party CDN, and processing runs in your browser. Broward360 does not upload the image to its own server, but do not use sensitive documents in this prototype.
